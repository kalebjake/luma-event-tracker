# Starting topic sets

Pick 5 to 8 topics that fit the calendar you're actually looking at. These sets are starting points, not fixed lists: drop topics nothing on the calendar matches, rename them to the audience's own vocabulary, and add one if a cluster of events doesn't fit anywhere. Keys are short and lowercase; labels are what people see.

## Crypto and onchain finance conferences

| key | label | fits |
|---|---|---|
| `institutional` | Institutional | TradFi, banks, asset managers, finance executives, family offices |
| `tokenization` | Tokenization & RWA | Real-world assets, tokenized funds and securities |
| `payments` | Payments & Stablecoins | Payments, stablecoins, money movement, fintech infrastructure |
| `developer` | Technical / Developer | Hackathons, workshops, validators, protocol engineering |
| `defi` | DeFi & Trading | DeFi protocols, trading, market structure, liquidity |
| `capital` | Founders & Capital | Demo days, founder and VC events, fundraising |
| `networking` | Networking & Social | Parties, breakfasts, meetups with no clear topic |
| `culture` | Culture & Gaming | Art, NFTs, gaming, music, consumer |

## Tagging rules

- One or two topics per event, most relevant first. Two only when the event really serves both audiences.
- Tag from the description and audience, not the host's brand. A payments company's breakfast with no agenda is Networking first.
- Use `networking` (or the set's social topic) for events whose description names no subject beyond meeting people.
